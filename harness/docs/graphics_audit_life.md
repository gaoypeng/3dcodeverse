# Living and animated graphics audit

> Historical working notes from the development snapshot (2026-09-23). The per-module test files
> and case counts cited below were folded into `tests/scene_runtime/lib/test_library.py` when the
> library landed on main (D96, D97); `/tmp/...` evidence paths are from that session and are not kept.

Audit started 2026-09-23 07:29 UTC against the current working tree. The first sections preserve the baseline assessment; implementation and validation below describe the completed changes. No shared gallery configuration was edited by this family owner.

The frozen source, source hashes, probe script/results, render scenes, PNGs, runtime logs and full test output are under `/tmp/graphics-seven-hour/baseline/life`. Every case contains its own copy of the frozen library so later shared-workspace edits cannot alter the baseline. The tests completed with **148 passed in 27.93 seconds**. Compilation and structural tests pass; this does not establish realism or complete resource cleanup.

The audit uses the production Three.js host and post chain at 960 × 640, times 0 and 0.7 seconds. Most scenes adapt the existing compile fixtures, adding a background or closer camera where needed. They are diagnostic scenes, not claimed polished showcases. The woodland compile fixture places cards closer than its own documented 35 m exclusion distance; `woodland_far` is the appropriate additional visual comparison. `meadow_showcase` uses the already authored meadow example for a composed reference. A sparse test fixture or missing scene ground is not attributed to a library defect.

## Reproducible defects and validation gaps

`probes.json` records the lifecycle inventory, the flock speed measurement and the shoe-height probe; the exact executable script is `src/audit_probe.mjs`. Fire and meadow both report one owned InstancedMesh and zero instance-disposal events in the minimal probes. Installed Three r182 `WebGLObjects.js` removes instanceMatrix/instanceColor buffers in its InstancedMesh dispose listener, independently of BufferGeometry disposal.

Most older factory tests are strong on seeded attributes, shader strings, counts and compilation. New tests should verify the physical or lifecycle behavior being changed: disposal of owned buffers and textures exactly once; caller-resource survival; finite-difference normals; light/shadow response; actual speed and foot contact; same-time rewind. Replacing one asserted shader string with another is insufficient.

At the frozen baseline, shared `shadowLike` supplied customDepthMaterial only. A shared customDistanceMaterial improvement could benefit several modules and should be coordinated with the owner of shader.js; independent per-module copies would duplicate sensitive Three.js shader integration. Flower alpha silhouettes also need the same cut in any new distance pass.

## Proposed implementation order

1. **Ownership and compatibility:** one agreed reusable ownership helper or small explicit snapshots, including textures, custom shadow materials and InstancedMesh buffers. Add update aliases without removing tick. Figure materials remain borrowed; patched materials remain caller-owned.
2. **Measurable motion and lighting:** flock arc-length speed, figure stance contact/zero speed, dapple receiver projection/normals, wind normals and leaf transmission. Test each behavior on controlled fixtures before aesthetic tuning.
3. **Botanical silhouette:** flower leaves/head variation, reed leaves/seedheads and canopy spray structure; inspect matched close and wide views, motion and backlighting. Keep instance/draw budgets visible.
4. **New capability:** a seeded branch-aware `makeTree` / `makeShrub` module, with tapered branch junctions, leaf sprays located at branch tips, optional age/species presets and coherent branch wind. Existing canopy creates only a leaf cloud, and existing imposters only distant cards. This fills the missing near/mid-tree role without duplicating grass, falling particles or fog.

A buoyant smoke/steam emitter is also valuable, particularly with fire/candles, but should be coordinated with the air-family work. Existing watermist and atmospheric fog are surface/region veils, not a rising turbulent plume. Prefer one well-tested emitter with emission history, bounded lifetime and scene lighting over several superficially renamed particle presets.

Any approved implementation should retain these frozen cameras/sources for comparison, add composed ecological scenes where the compile fixtures are insufficient, and reserve time for integration/GPU checks rather than consuming the full seven hours on optional species.

### Measurement precision

The initial shoe probe reported a conservative transformed bounding-box penetration of 5.44 cm. A later **exact vertex** probe on the frozen source measured 3.364 cm of actual shoe penetration and up to 2.208 cm with both feet above ground. The same exact probe on the new source reports ground error below 3e-16 m and a maximum swing lift of 11.18 cm. These are procedural fixture measurements, not motion-capture validation. The scripts/results are `/tmp/graphics-seven-hour/life_measure.mjs`, `baseline-foot-vertices.json`, and `after/life/probes.json`.

## New connected tree and shrub library

`tree.js` exports `makeTree(opts)` and `makeShrub(opts)`. Oak, birch and willow presets have connected primary limbs, secondary branches, twigs, fine shoots, real petioles and individually instanced species-shaped leaves. Primary radius follows its tapered parent's cross-section. Variable curvature, apical continuation, branch collars and buttress roots replace equal-weight repeated Y forks. Bark has procedural plate/fissure color and a filtered continuous height gradient; no generated bitmap or model was used. Leaf age/autumn variation, scene-lit transmission and coherent rooted wind are included.

The API uses nominal `height` and `crownRadius`, seeded `leafDensity`, `leafSize`, `maxLeaves`, `autumn`, colors, shadows and the existing wind vocabulary. `maxLeaves` thins the entire crown without stripping later-created branches. `userData.bounds` gives the actual local envelope, `branches` exposes parent/attachment/sample information, `leafAttachments` locates botanical anchors, and `sampleWind(point,t)` samples the wood deformation. These are deterministic absolute-time operations.

Each factory uses two geometry draws, one if bare, excluding shadow passes. Default oak is **961,224 triangles / 15,540 leaves**, birch **1,130,544 / 14,504**, willow **954,060 / 17,765**, and shrub **734,010 / 11,700**. This is a near/mid asset; leaf density/cap should be reduced for background use. The composed `examples/graphics_lab/scenes/tree.js` contains three species, a shrub, meadow, rock and a small projected distant treeline. Its independently counted total is **2,771,713 triangles and 14 geometry draws**. The frozen older census reports 918 fewer triangles because it misses the far cards' instance multiplier; the integrated runtime corrects that count.

Final source and hashes are frozen in `/tmp/graphics-seven-hour/new/tree/final`; renders at 1280 × 800 and t=0/.7 are in `final/renders`. The identical woodland/branches cameras were retained through v1–v11 iterations, so branch refinements are reviewable. Final GPU tests cover fog, directional plus point shadows, nested nonuniform transforms, actual pixel motion and exact time rewind. Structural tests verify branch and petiole attachment, whole-crown cap distribution, outward wood winding, bounds, seeded equality, invalid options and borrowed-resource survival.

Remaining limits are explicit: wind is a coherent deformation field rather than a dynamic beam solver; the host AO sees real tree/meadow geometry in its rest pose; standalone `patchWind` cannot install moving shadow materials on meshes it does not own; imposters remain distant cards; the figure remains a mannequin; fire retains its documented transparent-intersection and logarithmic-depth limits. None of the images is claimed to establish universal photorealism.
