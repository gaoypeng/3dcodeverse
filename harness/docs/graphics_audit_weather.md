## Evidence and method

The source snapshot, matching test files, executable lighting probe, staged scenes, original PNG frames, and render metrics are under `/tmp/graphics-seven-hour/baseline/weather/`. The snapshot contains the complete library to make every scene reproducible. The two kinds of scenes are deliberately separated:

- `fixtures/<module>` renders the module's existing shader fixture. These primarily establish compilation and basic behavior; several fixtures have black backgrounds or no environment and are unsuitable for judging final composition.
- `representative/<case>` adds controlled daytime or night lighting, a physical sky, an environment, and useful geometry. `surface_patches` compares individual material effects on a floor, box and spheres; `cloud_sky` exposes distant cloud silhouettes; `rain_shelter` exposes rainfall around and underneath a roof; `heat_shimmer` provides a structured background.

Renders use the actual production GPU host with its default postprocessing, 960 × 600, absolute times 0 and 1.5 seconds. Metrics identify the RTX 5090 Laptop GPU through ANGLE. The image `audit-contact.jpg` is an overview, not the sole evidence: individual images were also inspected. Initial staging failures from symlink restrictions and unexpanded fixture `__FOG__` tokens were corrected in temporary staging; they were not library failures. The completed renders have no console or shader errors. The 15 corresponding test files pass: **182 tests in 23.90 seconds**. This does not establish visual realism. The final evidence inventory contains **46 PNG frames across 14 staged render scenes**, with hashes in `manifest.json`.

Priorities: **P1** is a clear visual or correctness defect worth addressing in this work period; **P2** is a useful improvement with a functioning existing implementation; **P3 / retain** means no immediate replacement is justified by the inspected evidence.

## Bounded priorities for the seven-hour work period

1. Repair clear correctness and ownership defects first: hidden/removed light handling, godray coordinate mismatch, zero-amplitude puddles, framebuffer lifetime and active-target sizing. Add behavioral regressions rather than tests that only search shader strings.
2. Improve the conspicuous existing cloud and rain-veil appearance against the preserved views. Their silhouettes are larger realism failures than missing tiny details elsewhere.
3. Add one substantial **cloud volume** capability with a stable local-coordinate box, deterministic noise, Beer extinction, sun transmittance, a bounded ray budget and owned-resource teardown. Keep the current distant-cloud option for cheap background use. Explicitly state that this is prescribed density, not weather simulation.
4. If the time budget permits, add **surface-aware rainfall** with a static height field and synchronized impacts. Height-field support handles terrain and roofs with a single highest surface per XZ; overhang interiors and moving geometry require stronger collision data and should not be advertised as solved.
5. Improve material coating realism using masked roughness and filtered normal detail where the existing geometry is already adequate. Do not spend the entire period rewriting all 15 modules, or confuse snow albedo with accumulated snow geometry.

Deferred capabilities with genuine value are depth-aware participating-media lighting and geometric snow caps. Existing snowfall, spray, caustic projection, aurora, stars, rain rings, heat shimmer and puddles should not be relabeled as missing new effects.

### New bounded cloud volume

`cloudvolume.js` exports `makeCloudVolume(options)`. It returns a local, centered `THREE.Mesh` with `userData.update(t)`, `sampleDensity(x,y,z,t)`, `setSunDirection(direction)` and `dispose()`.

- `size: [x,y,z]` uses local metres; default `[600,180,360]`. `wind: [x,z]` uses local metres/second and absolute time. `seed` controls a periodic 80³ RGBA density texture (2,048,000 bytes before driver allocation).
- `coverage` and `detail` range from 0 to 1; `density` is extinction per world metre. The local-to-world path metric preserves optical thickness under nonuniform scale and composed shear.
- `quality: 'low' | 'balanced' | 'high'` selects 40/6, 72/10 or 112/16 view/sun integration steps without changing the underlying field. Secondary sun integration supplies self-shadowing. Beer transmittance accumulates front to back; a bounded multiple-scattering approximation lifts dense interiors.
- Lighting follows the strongest visible directional light and ambient/hemisphere/environment fill. Hidden/removed lights reset. `sunDirection` pins a world-space direction; the setter supports changes without rebaking density.
- This is prescribed animated density, not a weather/fluid simulation. It has ordinary transparent-object sorting, no terrain cloud-shadow projection, no scene-shadow-map sampling, no temporal reconstruction, and no scene-depth-aware termination inside its bounds. Opaque geometry wholly in front works through depth testing; opaque geometry intersecting the box can reject the whole cloud fragment. Use separate distant sky volumes.

`examples/graphics_lab/scenes/cloud_volume.js` composes three cloud volumes over a reflected mountain lake. Frozen final source and images are in `weather-refine/cloudvolume-final/`: lake, close cloud, and camera-inside views at 0 and 7 seconds, 1200 × 750, production postprocessing, zero shader/console errors. `cloudvolume-fractal-a/` preserves the earlier density experiment; the selected field replaces visibly smooth ellipsoid clusters with eroded multiscale structure. Tier comparisons use the exact same close camera at `cloudvolume-quality-{low,balanced,high}/`. Two-second host throughput samples were approximately 813/658/519 FPS on the RTX 5090 Laptop GPU; these are scene/device-specific short measurements, not guaranteed performance or mobile budgets.

Five module tests check seeded density bytes, absolute-time replay, CPU/GPU texture sampling agreement, boundary behavior, transformed camera/light transport, hidden/removed lights, all three actual GPU programs, camera-inside rendering, foreground geometry, pixel motion/replay, and GPU 3D-texture cleanup. Exact replay compares rendered bytes, not merely uniform values.

### Optional bounded rain shelter atlas

`makeRain({surfaces, heightAt, groundY, shelterResolution, shelterUpdateDistance, ...})` retains its existing lightweight path when no surfaces/height callback are supplied. `surfaces` are borrowed `Object3D` roots; `heightAt(x,z)` returns a local height or `null`. `groundY` supplies an optional flat local cutoff even without an atlas.

The atlas records the highest receiving height at each local XZ texel. It follows the camera when `follow` is enabled and covers the rain radius plus an update margin. Resolution is bounded to 8–64 per side (default 32): each rebuild uses at most resolution² downward ray queries, whose cost still depends on supplied mesh complexity. It rebuilds initially, after sufficient camera movement, after effect-transform changes, or on the next draw after `userData.refreshShelter()`. Moving, modifying or hiding a source mesh requires that explicit refresh. `userData.shelter` exposes bounds, texel size and rebuild count; `sampleShelterHeight(x,z)` reports cached local height or `null` outside/unbuilt coverage.

The leading edge of each rain streak disappears at the highest roof/ground height. This is a vertical height-field approximation: it does not solve wind entering laterally under eaves, layered overhang interiors, moving-body collisions, or synchronization of visible drops with independently phased splash sprites. Narrow roofs can be missed between texels. The factory disposes its atlas but never source geometry/materials.

`rain-shelter-matched/` preserves the original roof case with only the opt-in shelter configuration added, at its original camera/dimensions/times. `rain-shelter-composed-v2/` is a separate wet courtyard demonstration with a pitched shelter, dry deck and surface splashes. Roof/ground/camera behavior has both CPU and actual GPU tests. The composed deck exposed small regular dark postprocessing dots: they remain with `receiveShadow=false`, remain after both rain and splash objects are removed, and remain without the reflector; they disappear with `--no-post`. Those isolated copies are `rain-shelter-no-shadow`, `rain-shelter-no-effects` and `rain-shelter-no-floor`. This is a shared postprocessing investigation, not evidence that precipitation leaks into the depth pass; the runtime owner has the reproducer.

## Second visual pass and final snapshot

The subsequent visual pass changes the items explicitly left open in the first ledger:

- **Snow:** default albedo is nearly neutral, its tonal modulation is narrower, and deep deposits become opaque. Blue shadow color now comes primarily from scene illumination rather than large blue albedo patches. A filtered 0.9 mm height signal perturbs the lit normal without changing geometry. A real GPU albedo test verifies neutrality, adequate brightness and resolved tonal variation.
- **Aurora:** jittered Gaussian strands replace the hard, evenly spaced strokes. Each strand has its own brightness and upper extent, broader folds modulate the curtain, and the lower boundary fades unevenly. The folded normal uses the shared inverse transpose under parent transforms. This remains a luminous ribbon approximation, not a charged-particle simulation.
- **Shore foam:** world-anchored triplanar noise replaces projection onto a spatially changing tangent. On a centered sphere the former coordinates collapsed one axis, producing repeated stripe fans. The new field retains variation around the full curved shore; a GPU sampling regression measures that variation. Filtering reduces unresolved bubble detail.
- **Moss and mud:** filtered local height signals supply small moss/lichen relief and curled mud-lip shading. Narrower mud cracks reduce the thick graphic outlines, with less baked highlight color now that actual normals respond to light. `amount: 0` also removes moss's formerly unmasked damp-rim darkening. These remain surface coatings without silhouette changes.
